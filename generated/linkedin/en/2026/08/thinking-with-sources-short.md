A note is only reusable if we keep track of where the idea originated.

Recording the source, its context, and the date may feel slower, but it prevents future readers from getting lost.

This blog’s core rule is that the original post stays as Markdown; every derivative records the file and revision it came from. Shortening an idea never erases its history.

Metadata such as tags, archive links, RSS feeds, or translations makes the post discoverable later; it isn’t administrative overhead but a map for later readers.

When the source changes, its shorter derivatives need a fresh review. Automation can speed drafting, but it cannot assume responsibility for the writing. Responsibility for clarity stays with the writer, not the tool.
